## 3. THE STUDIO RULE OF THREE (every reel must hit at least one)

A piece earns its slot only if it does ONE of these three things:

1. **Names a loop** the viewer is currently inside (and didn't have a word for)
2. **Bears witness** to a moment the viewer thought no one saw
3. **Bridges ancient wisdom to a 2026 phone screen** in a way that doesn't sound like a Headspace ad

If it does none of the three → kill it. If it does two → ship it. If it does all three → it's a flagship.

## 4. THE BILLION-DOLLAR BAR (what we will not compromise on, ever)

| # | The bar | What it costs us | Why we pay it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Locked character portrait sheets** — every protagonist has 5 angles × 3 emotions, generated once at premium quality, used as image-to-image reference for every subsequent keyframe | 1 day of upfront work | Without it, every reel has face drift. With it, viewers recognize Samir on frame 1, anywhere. This is the Pixar move. |
| 2 | **One ≥1.5s silence per reel** (mandatory) | Sometimes makes a reel feel "slow" in editor preview | The silence is the moment the viewer feels seen. No silence = no recognition = no save. |
| 3 | **Captions Anton 140pt gold #FFD700 at Y=82%** (locked .ass template, never CapCut defaults) | We cannot use auto-caption tools | Auto-captions are how we look like every other AI-slop account. The locked template is how we look like a studio. |
| 4 | **Loop IDs backstage only** (never spoken, never on screen) | We can't do "lookup my loop" gimmicks | Codes feel clinical. "The 2 AM refresh" feels like recognition. We are in the recognition business. |
| 5 | **One real anchor per reel** — a real time, a real number, a real city, a real object | Slows down ideation | Specificity is the only moat against AI-generated wellness slop. A real time on a still photo > a generic 4K reel. |
| 6 | **Voice matched to protagonist** (ElevenLabs voice ID locked per character, not narrator-generic) | More VO setup per reel | Samir always sounds like Samir. Maya always sounds like Maya. Brand = consistent inner voice across 500 reels. |
| 7 | **The send-to-one-friend test** (would the protagonist send this to their best friend without flinching?) | Kills 30% of drafts | The 30% we kill is what would have made us look mid. |

## 5. THE WEEKLY STUDIO RHYTHM (locked production cadence)

```
MON  — Story Room: 5 protagonists × 1 loop each = 5 briefs written
TUE  — Keyframe Day: 30 keyframes generated (5 reels × 6 frames), all using locked portrait sheets
WED  — Clip Day: 30 video clips rendered (image-to-video from Tuesday's keyframes)
THU  — Voice Day: 5 VO scripts recorded via ElevenLabs (correct voice ID per protagonist) + music beds
FRI  — Compose Day: ffmpeg pipeline runs, captions burned, QA scorecards filled, 5 reels delivered
SAT  — Schedule Day: 5 reels queued across the week (1/day, 11:47 AM IST primary slot)
SUN  — Reflection: read DMs, pull 1 real user wound into next week's Samir reel
```

**5 reels/week × 50 weeks = 250 reels/year.** At 2× pace once the pipeline is hot, we hit 500 in 12 months.

## 6. THE FIVE FORBIDDEN MOVES (auto-kill at the door, no debate)

1. **Faceless wellness imagery** (orbs, gradients, faceless silhouettes) — this is what every AI-slop account does
2. **Captions auto-burned by CapCut/Submagic defaults** — instant brand death
3. **A reel with no protagonist code in its filename** — if it's "for everyone," it's for no one
4. **A reel where the loop is named on screen as "VAL004"** — codes stay backstage, always
5. **Any closing line that says "follow for more" / "save this for later" / "share with someone who needs this"** — these are the marks of a creator. We are a studio. Studios don't beg.
