## Post 8.1 — Burnout is a 21st-century word for a 5,000-year-old condition

Modern medicine got the official ICD-11 code for burnout in 2019.

The Yoga Vasistha mapped the same condition in detail before the year 1000 — they called it *chitta-vikshepa*, the scattering of consciousness. The cure was specific: 40 minutes of contemplative withdrawal per day, with structured re-entry.

Our app's protocol for burnout is, structurally, the same. We just put it in a state machine.

## Post 8.2 — Anxiety is not a chemical imbalance. It is a relationship imbalance.

Specifically: a broken relationship with the present moment.

You are anxious because you are time-traveling to a future that has not happened, and your body is responding as if it already did.

The cure the rishis prescribed: *one full breath, fully felt*. The neuroscience confirms this — a single 8-second exhale measurably reduces amygdala activation (Ma et al., 2017, *Frontiers in Psychology*).

Not a 10-day retreat. One breath. Right now.

— Vatsal

## Post 8.3 — Phone addiction is not a willpower problem

Every notification trains your brain to crave the next notification. The dopaminergic reward circuit you are using to scroll is the same one that handles substance addiction (Volkow et al., 2017, *Neuropharmacology*).

Willpower will not fix this. The only thing that fixes it is *competing reward* — something that reliably gives the nervous system a deeper hit than a notification.

A 60-second witness practice gives the right kind of user a deeper hit than a notification. We have data on this. Hard to believe until you experience it.

— Vatsal

## Post 8.6 — Imposter syndrome is the loop the Buddha specifically named

In Buddhist psychology, *upādāna* is clinging — including clinging to a self-concept you are afraid you can't sustain.

Imposter syndrome is upādāna with a modern wrapper. The cure is the same: notice the clinging. The clinging dissolves the moment it is seen clearly.

This sounds too simple. It is too simple. That is why it works.

— Vatsal

## Post 8.7 — Doomscrolling is just dukkha with a screen

The Buddha's first noble truth: *dukkha* — unsatisfactoriness. The mind compulsively seeks the next thing because no thing satisfies.

Doomscrolling is dukkha applied to news. Each headline promises a hit of meaning, delivers anxiety, the mind seeks the next headline.

The cure is identical to the original: bring full attention to one thing for 90 seconds. The compulsion breaks itself when seen.

— Vatsal
