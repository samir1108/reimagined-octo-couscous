# Source Bibliography — Pillar 3 (Ancient × Neuroscience)
## Every claim used in a post must be traceable to one of these. If it's not here, rewrite it as a personal observation, not a fact.

> Curated by The Neuroscientist + The Rishi. No paper = no claim.

---

## A. Default Mode Network ↔ Chitta Vritti

1. **Brewer JA et al. (2011)** — "Meditation experience is associated with differences in default mode network activity and connectivity." *PNAS* 108(50): 20254–20259. → Experienced meditators show reduced DMN activity (PCC, mPFC) during meditation AND at rest.
2. **Garrison KA et al. (2015)** — "Effortless awareness: using real time neurofeedback to investigate correlates of posterior cingulate cortex activity in meditators' self-report." *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 9:440. → PCC deactivation correlates with self-reported "effortless awareness."
3. **Patanjali Yoga Sutra 1.2** — *yogaś-citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ* — "Yoga is the cessation of the modifications of mind-stuff." (~200 BCE; tr. Bryant, 2009)

**Bridge claim allowed:** "Patanjali's *chitta vritti* is, mechanistically, what we now measure as Default Mode Network chatter."

## B. Vagal Tone, HRV ↔ Pranayama / Bhramari

1. **Porges SW (2007)** — "The polyvagal perspective." *Biological Psychology* 74(2): 116–143. → Ventral vagal complex governs social engagement & calm.
2. **Streeter CC et al. (2012)** — "Effects of yoga on the autonomic nervous system, gamma-aminobutyric-acid, and allostasis in epilepsy, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder." *Medical Hypotheses* 78(5): 571–579. → Yoga increases HRV and GABA.
3. **Kuppusamy M et al. (2018)** — "Effects of Bhramari Pranayama on health – A systematic review." *Journal of Traditional and Complementary Medicine* 8(1): 11–16. → Humming (Bhramari) raises nasal nitric oxide 15-fold; reduces sympathetic arousal.
4. **Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.68** (~15th c.) — Bhramari kumbhaka described.

## C. Interoception ↔ Antar Mauna / Witness

1. **Craig AD (2009)** — "How do you feel — now? The anterior insula and human awareness." *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 10: 59–70. → Right anterior insula = seat of interoceptive awareness.
2. **Farb NAS et al. (2013)** — "Mindfulness meditation training alters cortical representations of interoceptive attention." *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 8(1): 15–26.
3. **Ashtavakra Gita 1.4** (~5th c. BCE) — *sākṣī sarvasya* — "You are the witness of all."
4. **Mandukya Upanishad, verse 7** — *turīya* (the fourth state) — pure witnessing consciousness.

2. **Patanjali Yoga Sutra 2.29** — the 8 limbs (yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, samadhi).

2. **Clark A (2013)** — "Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science." *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 36(3): 181–204.
3. **Yoga Sutra 2.12–2.14** — *karmāśaya* (storehouse of impressions) and *saṃskāra* drive future experience.
4. **Vivekachudamani v.108–110** (Adi Shankara, ~8th c.) — *vāsanā* as the residue that conditions perception.

**Bridge claim allowed:** "*Samskaras* are the priors. *Vasana* is the prior's pull. Liberation is reducing prediction error to zero."

## Strict rules for using this bibliography

1. Cite the **finding**, not the paper title, in the post. ("PCC deactivates during effortless awareness — Garrison 2015.")
2. Never claim a paper says more than it says. If unsure → use as personal observation.
3. The Sanskrit term must be transliterated with diacritics in the strategy file, but in posts we drop diacritics for readability (use "samskara" not *saṃskāra*).
4. If a post needs a bridge claim not on this list, add the source here first. No source = no post.
